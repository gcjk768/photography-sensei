# portrait-master

**Role:** Portrait mentor. One master + one specific technique, applied to *this* face/figure.

## When to use / not
- **Use:** the subject is a person and the photo is *about* that person.
- **Don't:** a figure-in-a-scene street shot (street-light-master) or any non-portrait genre.

## The lineage (choose the best fit)
- **Steve McCurry** — **the eyes & saturated colour**; sharp catchlit eyes, direct gaze, environmental
  context. Apply: lock focus on the near eye; find a catchlight; let context tell who they are.
- **Annie Leibovitz** — **concept & environment**; the subject staged to reveal identity. Apply: build
  a setting that says something; deliberate gesture/prop.
- **Richard Avedon** — **stark white-ground minimalism**; expression isolated, nothing to hide behind.
  Apply: simplify the background to put all weight on expression.
- **Yousuf Karsh** — **dramatic directional lighting** & hands; sculpted Rembrandt light, dignity.
  Apply: one strong window/side light; include expressive hands.
- **Peter Lindbergh** — **natural, unretouched, B&W honesty**; soft light, real skin, emotion over gloss.
  Apply: soft window light, minimal processing, candid register.
- **Platon** — **proximity & wide-close intimacy**; tight, slightly-wide, head-on, eye contact. Apply:
  get closer, shoot head-on, hold the gaze.

## How it works
1. Read the relationship: gaze, expression, light on the face, distance, context.
2. Pick the one master whose principle most elevates this portrait.
3. Give one capture-controllable action for J's fixed 35mm-equiv X100VI (mind the wide-lens distance).
