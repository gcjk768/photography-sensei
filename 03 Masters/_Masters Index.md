# Masters Index — Who to Study, by Genre

A "who to study" reference for the Photo Sensei agent roster. Each master is paired with
the one lesson they teach best. Names in [[wikilinks]] have a full master note; plain
names are stubs to be written as the lineage is needed.

J's core lineage lives at the top: [[Fan Ho]] (light & geometry) and [[Saul Leiter]]
(colour & glass). Everything else is context and contrast.

---

## Street / Light

| Master | What they teach |
|---|---|
| [[Fan Ho]] | Geometry of light beams, diagonals, lone figure for scale, patience for the decisive light. **J's #1 lineage.** |
| [[Saul Leiter]] | Colour-as-abstraction, shooting through rain-streaked glass, reflections, telephoto compression, muted palettes. **J's #2 lineage.** |
| Henri Cartier-Bresson | The decisive moment — anticipating gesture and geometry to release at the perfect instant. |
| Daido Moriyama | Raw, high-contrast, grainy, blurred "are-bure-boke" — emotion over technical perfection. |
| André Kertész | Quiet poetry, distortion, and seeing the unexpected composition in the everyday. |

## Landscape

| Master | What they teach |
|---|---|
| Ansel Adams | The Zone System — previsualising and controlling tonal range from deep black to pure white. |
| Galen Rowell | "Dynamic landscape" — chasing fleeting mountain light, weather, and the photographer-on-the-move. |
| Michael Kenna | Minimalist long exposures, negative space, and the stillness of small square prints. |
| Sebastião Salgado | Epic monochrome grandeur — landscapes and humanity at planetary scale, with deep tonal drama. |

## Portrait

| Master | What they teach |
|---|---|
| Steve McCurry | Arresting eye contact, saturated colour, and the single human face that holds a whole story. |
| Annie Leibovitz | Conceptual, environmental portraits — elaborate staging that reveals character. |
| Richard Avedon | Stark white seamless, unflinching honesty, and the subject stripped of context. |
| Yousuf Karsh | Dramatic Rembrandt lighting and gravitas — the dignified, sculpted portrait. |
| Peter Lindbergh | Natural, cinematic black-and-white — beauty without artifice, honesty over retouching. |
| Platon | Tight, frontal, wide-lens close-ups that confront the viewer with raw presence. |

## Macro

| Master | What they teach |
|---|---|
| Andrew Biss | Surreal, painterly close-up worlds — mood and narrative built at tiny scale. |
| Thomas Shahan | Extreme insect macro with personality — eye-level portraits of jumping spiders and bugs. |

## Wildlife

| Master | What they teach |
|---|---|
| Andy Rouse | Patient fieldcraft and behaviour — getting close ethically and reading the animal. |
| Marina Cano | Dramatic light and motion in wildlife — animals as graphic, emotive subjects. |
| Tim Flach | Studio-style animal portraiture — wildlife shot with the intent of human portraiture. |

## Architecture

| Master | What they teach |
|---|---|
| Julius Shulman | Mid-century modern staged for life — lifestyle and light selling the building. |
| Hélène Binet | Light, shadow, and texture as the true subject — architecture as abstract form. |
| Iwan Baan | Buildings in their human and social context — the life around the structure. |

## Astro

| Master | What they teach |
|---|---|
| Babak Tafreshi | "The World at Night" — landscape and the night sky composed together as one image. |

## Still-life

| Master | What they teach |
|---|---|
| Irving Penn | Precision, simplicity, and exquisite control of light on objects against plain grounds. |
| Paulette Tavormina | Old-master painterly still-life — chiaroscuro, arrangement, and lush low light. |

## Food

| Master | What they teach |
|---|---|
| Andrew Scrivani | Natural-light, story-driven food — appetite and authenticity over slick gloss. |

## Sports

| Master | What they teach |
|---|---|
| Neil Leifer | The peak-action, perfectly-timed icon — anticipating the defining moment of a match. |
| Walter Iooss | Grace and geometry in motion — the athlete as elegant, composed form. |

## Abstract

| Master | What they teach |
|---|---|
| Aaron Siskind | Found abstraction — peeling walls and surfaces seen as flat, graphic compositions. |
| Ernst Haas | Colour, motion blur, and lyrical abstraction — painting with a camera. |
| Hiroshi Sugimoto | Conceptual minimalism and long-duration time — seascapes and theatres as meditations. |

## Documentary

| Master | What they teach |
|---|---|
| W. Eugene Smith | The immersive photo-essay — months of empathy distilled into a humane narrative. |
| Dorothea Lange | Dignity in hardship — the social document that moves policy and hearts. |
| James Nachtwey | Unflinching conflict witness — composition and courage in the face of suffering. |

## Travel

| Master | What they teach |
|---|---|
| Steve McCurry | Colour, culture, and the human face as the gateway to a place. |
| Jimmy Nelson | Formal, respectful portraits of vanishing cultures — collaboration and ceremony. |

## Long-exposure

| Master | What they teach |
|---|---|
| Michael Kenna | Serene, minimal long exposures — smoothing time into stillness. |
| Alexey Titarenko | "City of Shadows" — crowds blurred into ghostly rivers by very long exposures. |

## How J should use this index

- Start with the top of the **Street / Light** table — [[Fan Ho]] and [[Saul Leiter]] are
  J's spine. Read those two notes deeply before exploring outward.
- When a session critique mentions a technique (e.g. "this reads like compression"), follow
  the wikilink to the master who owns that lesson.
- Treat the other genres as **cross-training**: borrow Adams' tonal control, Leiter's colour,
  Siskind's found-abstraction eye — and bring them back to the 35mm street.
