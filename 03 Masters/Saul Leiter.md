# Saul Leiter

> "I don't have a philosophy. I have a camera." — and yet every frame is a small painting.

## Known for

- A **pioneer of colour street photography** at a time (late 1940s–50s) when serious
  photography meant black-and-white. He shot Kodachrome in the East Village decades before
  colour was respectable.
- A **painter first** — trained as an abstract expressionist — who carried that sensibility
  into the street: composition over reportage, mood over information.
- Largely unknown until a 2006 monograph (*Early Color*) and the documentary
  *In No Great Hurry* rediscovered him late in life.
- Quiet, intimate, weather-soaked New York: umbrellas, fogged windows, snow, taxis seen
  through glass — beauty found a few steps from his apartment.

## Signature principle(s)

These are the specific, nameable moves to learn from him:

- **Shooting through glass — rain-streaked, fogged, condensation-beaded.** The window is a
  filter that abstracts, softens, and adds a second layer of image between lens and subject.
- **Reflections as composition.** Shop windows, car panels, puddles, mirrors — he overlaps
  the reflected world with the real one until the two become a single ambiguous picture.
- **Telephoto compression.** He often worked with longer focal lengths (≈90–150mm), which
  flattens the scene, stacks the layers, and isolates a small slice of a chaotic street.
- **Foreground obstruction.** A blurred awning, a doorframe, a passing coat, a smear of
  green — something out-of-focus deliberately blocks part of the frame, creating depth and
  mystery and hiding as much as it reveals.
- **Muted, painterly palette.** Faded reds, dirty greens, warm browns, a single saturated
  accent in a hush of greys. Colour is *atmosphere*, never spectacle.
- **Colour-as-abstraction.** A red umbrella, a yellow taxi, a green door becomes a shape and
  a feeling first, a thing second — the picture works as pure colour-field composition.
- **Weather as collaborator — snow, steam, rain, condensation.** Bad weather is the point:
  it veils, diffuses, and unifies.
- **The off-centre, partial subject.** People are cropped, turned away, half-hidden, placed
  at the edge. The frame is mostly negative space and weather.

## Why J should study them

- Leiter **is J's #2 lineage** and the direct source of J's **shot-through-glass aesthetic** —
  the moody, layered, glass-and-reflection look J is already drawn to.
- Where [[Fan Ho]] teaches J the *graphic black-and-white architecture of light*, Leiter
  teaches J **colour, softness, and layering** — the other half of J's voice.
- He proves a single normal-to-short-tele lens, a small neighbourhood, and patience in bad
  weather are enough. No travel, no spectacle — just looking closely at the everyday.
- Singapore is humid, rainy, neon-lit, glass-walled — a near-perfect Leiter laboratory.

## What to borrow (apply to your shots)

- **Hunt rain-streaked and fogged glass.** Café windows, MRT doors, hawker-centre glass,
  bus windows on a wet day, car windscreens. Shoot *through* them. The X100VI's leaf shutter
  and quiet body make this candid work easy.
- **Find reflections deliberately.** Stand so a shop window or wet pavement reflects neon,
  a figure, or sky — then frame so the reflection and the real scene overlap and confuse.
- **Add a foreground obstruction.** Let a blurred pillar, plant, awning, or passing person
  occupy a third of the frame. On the X100VI, open up to **f/2–f/2.8** so the obstruction
  melts and the layer behind it stays sharp.
- **Mute the palette.** Try the **CLASSIC CHROME** or **Nostalgic Neg.** film simulation,
  drop highlights and saturation slightly, and look for one accent colour in a field of grey.
- **Embrace the 35mm as a "compression" exercise.** J can't zoom, so *move back* and let
  layers stack — shoot a scene with three depth planes (glass, mid, far) within the 35mm view.
- **Shoot in the weather J would normally avoid.** Tropical downpours, evening humidity haze,
  morning condensation on glass. The veil is the picture.
- **Crop the human in.** Let the subject be partial, edge-placed, turned away. Most of the
  frame should be weather, colour, and glass — the person is a quiet accent.
- **Title and sequence quietly.** Leiter never shouted. Keep edits hushed, warm, unhurried.

## J's drill

> A rainy evening, one glass surface (café, bus, or MRT door), and the 35mm wide open.
> Make three frames that each have a foreground obstruction, a reflection, and exactly
> one accent colour. No clean, unobstructed shots allowed.
