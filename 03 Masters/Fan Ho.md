# Fan Ho

> "I photograph with patience. I wait for the light to come to me."

## Known for

- The defining poet of 1950s–60s **Hong Kong street photography** — narrow alleys,
  tenement stairwells, wet markets, harbour mist.
- **"Approaching Shadow" (1954)** — his most iconic image: a lone girl against a sunlit
  wall, a hard diagonal of shadow sliding toward her. (He later admitted the diagonal
  was darkroom-printed in, not captured — a reminder that the *idea* leads.)
- A body of work that reads like **graphic design made from real life**: triangles of
  light, lone silhouettes, beams cutting through steam and smoke.
- Won over 280 international awards; also a film director. A self-taught technician who
  treated the darkroom as a second act of authorship.

## Signature principle(s)

These are the specific, nameable moves to learn from him:

- **Geometric beams of light.** He hunts the single shaft of sun that punches into a dark
  alley and lands as a clean shape. The light is the *subject*, not the illumination.
- **The dominant diagonal.** Stairs, cast shadows, ramps, hand-rails — a strong diagonal
  drags the eye across the frame and creates tension against the rectangle.
- **The lone figure for scale and story.** One small silhouette against a vast wall or
  beam gives the architecture its size and the picture its loneliness. People are
  punctuation, not crowds.
- **Atmosphere as a tool — smoke, steam, mist, dust.** He waited for, and sometimes
  engineered, haze so light beams become *visible volumes*, not flat patches.
- **High-contrast tonal printing.** Crushed blacks, glowing highlights, a deliberate gap
  in the mid-tones. The mood is built in the print as much as the capture.
- **Patience for the decisive light.** Not Cartier-Bresson's decisive *moment* of gesture,
  but the decisive *instant of illumination* — he would return to a spot repeatedly until
  the sun, the figure, and the geometry aligned.
- **Frame-within-a-frame.** Doorways, arches, railings, and shadow edges box the subject
  and layer the depth.

## Why J should study them

- Fan Ho **is J's #1 lineage.** A fixed normal lens (J's 35mm-equiv X100VI ≈ Fan Ho's
  working perspective), a single city, and light as the true subject — this is the same
  game J is already playing in Singapore.
- Singapore's HDB void decks, MRT underpasses, wet markets, and back-lanes are the modern
  analogue of 1960s Hong Kong alleys: hard tropical sun, deep shade, geometric concrete.
- He proves that **constraint breeds vision** — one lens, one city, decades of patience.
  J does not need new gear; J needs to return to the same corners until the light arrives.
- His moody, graphic, light-and-shadow language is precisely the aesthetic J is chasing.

## What to borrow (apply to your shots)

- **Expose for the highlight, let the shadows crush.** On the X100VI, dial in roughly
  −1 to −2 EV and meter the bright beam. Protect the highlight; the blacks become the
  frame's negative space. Try the **ACROS** film simulation (or ACROS+R for deeper skies).
- **Go shooting in the harsh midday-to-late-afternoon window**, not just golden hour.
  J wants the hard, raking shafts that slice through void decks and stairwells.
- **Find the beam first, then wait for a figure to walk into it.** Pre-focus on the lit
  patch, set a small aperture (f/5.6–f/8) for zone-focus depth, and wait. The picture is
  made when one person enters the light.
- **Build the diagonal.** Shoot up or down a staircase, along an escalator, or across a
  cast shadow so a strong diagonal organises the whole frame.
- **Hunt atmosphere.** Hawker-stall steam, incense smoke at temples, morning haze, rain
  evaporating off hot pavement — shoot *into* it with the light behind, so the air glows.
- **Use the lone-figure-for-scale rule:** if the architecture is huge, give it one small
  silhouette. If the frame feels empty, that emptiness *is* the subject.
- **Return.** Pick three Singapore locations (a stairwell, an underpass, a market lane) and
  shoot each one five times across different days and light. The fifth visit is the keeper.
