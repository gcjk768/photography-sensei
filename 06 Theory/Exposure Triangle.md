# Exposure Triangle

Three controls decide how bright your photo is, and each one changes a *second* thing besides brightness. That second thing is where the creativity lives.

## The three controls

| Control | Sets brightness by... | Side effect (the creative part) |
|---|---|---|
| **Aperture** (f-number) | size of the lens opening | depth of field — how much is in focus |
| **Shutter speed** | how long the sensor is exposed | motion — frozen or blurred |
| **ISO** | how much the signal is amplified | noise/grain — clean or gritty |

Lower f-number = bigger hole = more light + shallower focus. Faster shutter = less light + frozen motion. Higher ISO = brighter + noisier.

## Reciprocity (the trade)

Exposure is a fixed "bucket of light." If you halve the light from one control, you can double it on another and keep the same brightness. Each full step is called a **stop** — one stop = double or half the light.

- f/2 → f/2.8 = one stop *less* light (close the aperture)
- 1/125s → 1/250s = one stop *less* light (faster shutter)
- ISO 200 → ISO 400 = one stop *more* light (more amplification)

So f/2 at 1/500s ISO 200 gives the *same brightness* as f/2.8 at 1/250s ISO 200 — but the first has shallower focus, the second has more depth. You choose based on what the photo needs.

## On the X100VI

- **Aperture: f/2 to f/16.** f/2 for shallow focus and low light; f/5.6–f/8 for sharp, deep street scenes; avoid f/11–f/16 for critical sharpness (diffraction softens the 40MP sensor).
- **Leaf shutter** — quiet, no mirror slap, and it lets you flash-sync at *any* shutter speed (a real advantage over most cameras; see [[Fujifilm X100VI]]).
- **IBIS** lets you hand-hold at slower shutter speeds (down to ~1/15s with care) without ISO climbing — great for dim Singapore interiors and night streets. IBIS stops *camera* shake, not *subject* motion: a moving person still blurs.
- **Base ISO 125.** Keep ISO low when light allows; push it without fear up to ISO 3200–6400 — the JPEG noise reduction on the [[Classic Negative C7]] recipe handles it cleanly and the grain suits the moody look.

## Exposure compensation

The camera meters for a neutral mid-grey average, which it gets *wrong* for high-contrast scenes. The exposure-comp dial is your override:

- Bright/backlit scenes the camera makes too dark → dial **+** comp.
- For your moody light-and-shadow style, dial **−2/3 to −1 1/3** to let shadows go black and protect highlights. Underexposing slightly is how you get the [[Fan Ho]] / [[Saul Leiter]] look straight out of camera.
