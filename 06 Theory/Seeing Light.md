# Seeing Light

Photography is *drawing with light* (the word literally means that). Before you frame anything, learn to see the light that's already there — its direction and its quality. This is the single skill that separates snapshots from photographs.

## Direction

Where the light comes from changes everything:

- **Front light** (behind you, on the subject): flat, even, safe, a bit boring. Hides texture.
- **Side light** (from left/right): reveals texture and form, carves shadows, adds drama. The workhorse of moody work.
- **Backlight** (behind the subject): rim light, silhouettes, glowing edges, haze. High risk of blown highlights — meter for the highlight (see [[Reading the Histogram]]).
- **Top light** (overhead, e.g. equatorial noon): harsh, deep eye-socket shadows on faces — bad for portraits, but it makes hard graphic shadows on the ground that [[Fan Ho]] built a career on.

## Quality: hard vs soft

- **Hard light** = small or distant source (bare sun, a spotlight, a single window). Sharp-edged shadows, high contrast, drama. This is *your* light — the foundation of the [[Fan Ho]] / [[Saul Leiter]] lineage.
- **Soft light** = large or diffused source (overcast sky, light through a curtain, open shade). Gentle gradations, forgiving, flattering. Good for portraits and detail.

Bigger source relative to subject = softer. Move the subject to the light, or wait for the light to move.

## Singapore's equatorial light by time of day

The sun sits nearly overhead and the day is short on golden light — but Singapore has its own gifts:

- **~07:00 sunrise / ~19:00 sunset, year-round** (little seasonal drift). Golden hour is *brief* — maybe 30–40 min — so be in position early.
- **Golden hour** (first/last hour of sun): warm, low, raking side light. Long shadows down HDB corridors and through five-foot-ways. The best window for your style.
- **Mid-morning to mid-afternoon (~10:00–16:00):** brutal top light, contrasty, blue-white. Most people avoid it — *you* hunt it. This is when light beams cut through gaps and hard shadows fall as clean graphic shapes.
- **Blue hour** (~20 min after sunset / before sunrise): cool, even, glowing. City lights balance the deep blue sky. Great for cinematic streetscapes.
- **The afternoon storm:** Singapore's daily downpour resets the light — wet reflective ground, dramatic skies, then soft post-rain glow. Reflections everywhere ([[Saul Leiter]] territory).

## Finding light beams

The signature move: hard sunlight slicing through a gap (between buildings, under an overhead bridge, through a void-deck slot, a doorway, a slatted wall) into shadow. Look for the **bright wedge on a dark floor**, then wait for a figure to walk into it.

- Best in mid-morning / mid-afternoon when the sun is angled, not straight down.
- Expose for the **lit area** (−1 EV or more) so the surrounding shadow drops to black and the beam glows.
- Backlit dust, haze, or rain makes the beam itself visible.

## The masters

- [[Fan Ho]] — hard equatorial light, beams and shadow wedges, lone figures. Hong Kong's light is close to Singapore's; his playbook works here directly.
- [[Saul Leiter]] — soft, foggy, reflective light; colour through glass, rain, steam, and condensation. The opposite weather, equally moody.

## Practice mission

**"The light test."** Before pressing the shutter, say out loud (or note) the light's *direction* and *quality*. Shoot only when both serve the photo — reject flat front light. Find and shoot one light beam. Targets the **Light** dimension. (See [[_Mission Library]] → Fundamentals Track.)
