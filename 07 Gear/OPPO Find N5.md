# OPPO Find N5

J's always-with-you camera. A folding phone with a capable multi-camera system. The job here is to make phone frames that sit *next to* the [[Fujifilm X100VI]] shots without looking like a different photographer took them.

## The cameras

- **50MP main** (wide) — the workhorse; use this ~90% of the time.
- **50MP periscope tele** (~70mm-equiv, ~3x) — great for compression, isolation, and a portrait-length crop.
- **8MP ultra-wide** — useful but the weak link (see below).
- **Pro mode with RAW (DNG)** capture.

## Sensor reality (read this before you over-trust the phone)

Phone sensors are *physically tiny* compared to the X100VI's APS-C. Small sensors gather far less light, so the phone leans hard on **computational photography** — HDR stacking, multi-frame noise reduction, AI sharpening, and scene detection. The default "Photo" mode produces a punchy, over-processed, over-sharpened, HDR-flat look that fights J's moody style:

- Crushed-then-lifted shadows (kills the deep-black look).
- Aggressive sharpening and saturation.
- Skies and highlights "rescued" into a flat grey.

The computational engine is optimised to look good on a phone screen *to everyone* — not to look like Fan Ho.

## Shoot Pro mode + RAW for cohesion with the Fuji

To match the X100VI:

- **Use Pro mode**, capture **RAW (DNG)**. This bypasses most of the computational over-processing and gives you the data to grade it yourself.
- **Set white balance manually** (don't let Auto WB drift shot-to-shot) so a set stays cohesive.
- Keep ISO as low as the scene allows; the small sensor noises up fast.
- Then process in Lightroom with the matching **"FujiCN-ish"** preset in [[Singapore Recipe Set]] — built to mimic [[Classic Negative C7]]: muted colour, deep shadows, slight green/teal cast, restrained highlights. This is what makes phone + Fuji frames live in the same gallery.

## Expose for the highlights

Same JPEG/RAW logic as the Fuji's histogram lesson ([[Reading the Histogram]]): in Pro mode, **expose so the bright areas keep detail** and let shadows fall dark. A small sensor's highlights clip ugly. Drag exposure down (negative EV) — it suits the moody look *and* protects the sensor's weakest area. You'll lift the shadows you want back in Lightroom.

## Avoid the 8MP UW in dim light

The ultra-wide is only 8MP with a small aperture and a tiny sensor — it falls apart in low light (noisy, smeared, soft). Rules:

- Use the UW **only in good light** for graphic wide scenes (architecture, sky, sweeping streets).
- In dim conditions, switch to the **50MP main** and step back, or stitch — never the UW.
- For tight/compressed moody frames, reach for the **70mm tele** instead.

## Bottom line

Treat the Find N5 as a real camera with manual controls, not a point-and-shoot: Pro mode, RAW, expose for highlights, low ISO, main or tele lens, then grade to match the Fuji. See [[Singapore Recipe Set]].
