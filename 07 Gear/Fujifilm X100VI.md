# Fujifilm X100VI

J's primary camera. A fixed-lens compact built for one thing: being *with* you and disappearing into the street. The constraints are the point.

## Specs that matter

- **Sensor:** 40MP APS-C X-Trans CMOS 5 HR — huge resolution, lots of room to crop into a "tighter" framing without changing lenses.
- **Lens:** fixed **23mm f/2** = **35mm-equivalent** field of view. No zoom. f/2 → f/16.
- **IBIS:** in-body stabilisation (~6 stops) — new to this generation, a big deal for low-light hand-holding.
- **Leaf shutter:** near-silent, no vibration, and flash-syncs at **any** shutter speed.
- **Capture:** J shoots **JPEG only**, using the [[Classic Negative C7]] recipe (see also [[Singapore Recipe Set]]).
- **Base ISO 125**, clean to ~3200–6400.
- Built-in 4-stop **ND filter** — lets you shoot f/2 in bright sun, or drag the shutter for motion blur in daylight.

## One-focal-length discipline (35mm-equiv)

35mm is the "two steps back from normal" street length — wide enough for context, tight enough for a subject. Because it never changes, **you zoom with your feet**. This is a feature:

- You learn to *see* in 35mm — you'll start pre-visualising the frame before the camera is up.
- Composition decisions come from where you stand, not a zoom ring.
- One lens = no gear paralysis = more shooting. The 40MP sensor gives a built-in "crop zoom" to a ~50mm look when you truly need it.

This single-focal discipline is exactly how [[Fan Ho]] and the classic street masters worked.

## Protecting highlights in Singapore sun

Equatorial top light is harsh and JPEGs can't recover blown highlights (see [[Reading the Histogram]]). Defend at capture:

- **Use Dynamic Range / D Range Priority.** DR200 protects ~1 extra stop of highlight, DR400 ~2 stops (DR200 needs ISO ≥250, DR400 needs ISO ≥500). **D Range Priority (Auto/Strong)** is the easiest one-switch protection for contrasty scenes — it tames highlights *and* opens shadows in-camera.
- Dial **−2/3 EV** exposure compensation as standard insurance in bright scenes.
- Turn on **highlight blinkies** / watch the live histogram; protect the right edge first.
- Use the **built-in ND** to stay at f/2 in daylight without overexposing.

## IBIS

Hand-hold down to ~1/15s (with care) for dim void decks, MRT interiors, and blue-hour streets — keeping ISO low and the JPEG clean. Remember: IBIS steadies *the camera*, not the *subject* — a walking person still motion-blurs, which you can use creatively.

## Leaf-shutter flash sync

Most cameras cap flash at ~1/250s (X-sync). The X100VI's leaf shutter syncs flash at **every** speed up to 1/4000s — so you can use fill flash at f/2 in bright daylight to lift a backlit subject while keeping a dark, moody background. Pair with the built-in ND. This is a genuine creative superpower for hard-light portraits.
